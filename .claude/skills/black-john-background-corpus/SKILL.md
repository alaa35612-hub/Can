# Black John Background Corpus Protocol

## Mission
Use legacy Black John documents, pattern libraries, prompts and case notes as a searchable background corpus without granting them automatic authority.

## Corpus roles
The corpus may provide:
- terminology;
- candidate mechanisms;
- pattern families;
- expected sequences;
- alternative explanations;
- known failure modes;
- audit questions;
- historical cases requiring verification.

The corpus may not provide by itself:
- a final classification;
- a universal threshold;
- proof of institutional intent;
- campaign termination;
- a durable rule;
- historical detectability.

## Provenance requirements
Whenever corpus knowledge influences an analysis, record:
- source document and section;
- original claim;
- whether it is descriptive, numeric or causal;
- validation status;
- supporting project cases;
- contradictory project cases;
- adaptations made for symbol, timeframe, liquidity or regime.

## Legacy numeric rules
Preserve original numeric values for audit provenance, but translate them into adaptive research questions before use. Example:

Legacy claim: `Trades ratio >= 5 means Watch`.

Research question: Is current execution expansion exceptional relative to this symbol's causal historical distribution, does it persist, and is it confirmed by quote value, OI behavior and price acceptance?

## Legacy sample claims
Claims derived from OKX, Top Gainers or narrow 15-minute samples must be labeled `LIMITED_EXTERNAL_VALIDATION` until re-tested on the project's Binance data, failures and controls.

## Missing-sample rule
A symbol absent from a later ranked file is `NOT_OBSERVED_IN_CURRENT_SAMPLE`. Do not infer failure or campaign termination without direct temporal evidence.

## Pattern-card conversion
Convert each legacy pattern into a research card with:
- status;
- expected sequence;
- supporting and opposing evidence;
- discriminators;
- invalidation;
- validation status for Binance cases, controls and blind replay.

## Output
A corpus citation trail and an explicit statement of which claims were accepted, modified, restricted, rejected or left unverified.
