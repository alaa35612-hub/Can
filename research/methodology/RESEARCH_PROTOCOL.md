# Mandatory Forensic Structural Research Protocol

## Research question

Which ordered, causally observable structures distinguish campaigns that later expand upward from failed, late, noisy or ordinary periods, and which partial structures are currently preparing without already being overextended?

## Unit of analysis

The primary unit is a campaign, not a candle and not a symbol's final return. A symbol may contain multiple campaigns, resets, failures and rebuilds.

## Phase A — Independent campaign reconstruction

1. Load every available file for the symbol and preserve source provenance.
2. Validate data before interpretation.
3. Sort observations causally and remove only verified duplicates.
4. Establish symbol-local and timeframe-local historical context.
5. Identify ordered changes in price, OI/OI value, execution, positioning and acceptance.
6. Segment candidate campaigns while avoiding outcome-conditioned boundaries.
7. Record facts before naming mechanisms.
8. Generate competing hypotheses, including failure/noise and `NEW_UNIDENTIFIED_STRUCTURE`.
9. Attempt to falsify the dominant explanation.
10. Update the persistent State Ledger without rewriting prior states.

## Phase B — Validation and synthesis

1. Select successful, failed and matched ordinary controls.
2. Run blind replay from historical cutoffs.
3. Compare event ordering, persistence, confirmation and invalidation.
4. Audit old conclusions against data available at their original time.
5. Extract necessary evidence, supporting evidence, discriminators and failure filters.
6. Search for counterexamples before generalizing.
7. Assign an explicit lifecycle status to every rule.
8. Use validated results to assess current partial sequences.

## Evidence dimensions

- Price structure: compression, base, resilience, reclaim, breakout, acceptance, rejection and distance from footprint.
- Fuel: OI contracts, OI value, build, flush, reload, retention, churn and collapse.
- Execution: trades, quote volume, taker flow, average trade value, abnormality, acceleration and persistence.
- Positioning: Global L/S, Top Account L/S, Top Position L/S, divergence and crowd transition.
- Temporal context: campaign age, state transition, continuity, prior reset/failure and higher-timeframe context.
- Reliability: closure, coverage, alignment, missing fields, duplicate/gap risk and warm-up sufficiency.

## Adaptive interpretation

Analytical judgments must be relative to the asset, timeframe, liquidity, volatility, regime and causal historical distribution. Legacy numbers may be preserved as provenance but cannot act as universal gates.

## Mandatory adversarial questions

- What is the strongest alternative explanation?
- Does the same visible signature occur in failed or ordinary periods?
- Which observation led and which merely followed price?
- Is the claimed mechanism identifiable from available fields?
- Did any conclusion rely on future extrema or the known outcome?
- Would the conclusion survive removal of one indicator?
- Is the case truly new, or continuation/reload of an older campaign?

## Required final outputs

Complete factual timeline; campaign segmentation and State Ledger; competing hypothesis matrix; successful, failed and control analogues; prior-analysis corrections; blind-replay trace; rule lifecycle decisions; cross-coin synthesis; and current candidates with missing evidence and invalidation.
